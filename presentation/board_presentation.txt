[SLIDE TITLE]:
Sized for Our Real Scale, Not Amazon's

[SLIDE SUBTITLE]:
The Architecture That Fits Kloud Kart

[SLIDE BODY]:
LEFT COLUMN: THE MISTAKE WE ARE AVOIDING (OVER-ENGINEERING)
* Cloud Data Warehouses (Snowflake, BigQuery): Metered cloud storage and compute with unpredictable monthly bills.
* Data Lakehouses: High software complexity requiring dedicated data engineering teams that small companies cannot hire or afford.
* Our True Scale: Kloud Kart holds ~40 gigabytes of data. 40 gigabytes fits entirely inside computer memory; it does not justify heavy enterprise cloud tools.

RIGHT COLUMN: OUR PRACTICAL SOLUTION (TWO-SERVER ARCHITECTURE)
* Activate Pre-Approved Server 2: Turn on our pre-approved duplicate server as an automated, read-only mirror of the store database.
* Isolate Reporting Completely: Move all heavy reports off Server 1. Customers shopping online never experience checkout delays.
* Clean Reporting Area on Server 2: Store clean, pre-calculated reporting tables inside a dedicated area on that same second server, requiring zero new database servers.
* Single Source of Truth: Automated database queries replace untracked personal spreadsheets.

[SPEAKER NOTES FOR NANA ADJEI]:
"Investors often ask if we are building an unneeded cloud warehouse or buying Snowflake. The honest answer is that for a 40-gigabyte company, those tools are expensive over-engineering. We are using our already-budgeted second server to create a duplicate database for reporting. Shoppers use the store; reports read from the copy. Simple, reliable, and affordable."

[RECOMMENDED LAYOUT]:
Three-Step Security Pipeline Layout (Left: Gate Scrubbing | Middle: Anonymization | Right: Audit Logging)

[SLIDE TITLE]:
Protecting Customer Privacy and Meeting Our 90-Day Implementation Goal

[SLIDE SUBTITLE]:
Data Protection and Legal Defensibility (Ghana Act 843)

[SLIDE BODY]:
THE LEGAL CONTEXT (GHANA DATA PROTECTION ACT, 2012 / ACT 843):
* Section 33 gives individuals the statutory right to request erasure of their personal data (with standard turnaround guidelines requiring action within 21 calendar days).
* Kloud Kart has a strict 90-day internal implementation period to locate unmanaged files and resolve an active complaint forwarded by the Data Protection Commission regarding a customer's deleted data.
* The Core Rule: Every unmanaged spreadsheet you create is a place a customer deletion request must reach.

OUR THREE-PART COMPLIANCE DEFENSE:
1. Strip Personal Details at the Gate: Remove customer names, phone numbers, and physical delivery addresses before transactions enter the reporting layer.
2. Irreversible Anonymous Codes: Store customer emails as one-way anonymous reference codes (such as turning an email into `cust_98234`), protecting privacy while preserving accurate repeat-purchase analysis.
3. Permanent Deletion Audit Log & Dual-Path Protocol: Record every customer data request in a permanent audit log retained for six years (accessible only by Nana Adjei and the lead database engineer, with read-only inspection access for official auditors). This automatically re-applies deletions to restored backup files (removing), while permitting targeted recovery of order history via surrogate keys if an account was closed or contested in error (restoring).

[SPEAKER NOTES FOR NANA ADJEI]:
"Under Ghana's Data Protection Act, customers can legally demand that we delete their personal information. Today, because customer records sit on dozens of personal laptops, we cannot prove we have deleted everything. Our plan fixes this. We strip names, phone numbers, and addresses at the door before data enters the reporting system. We also log every action in a permanent audit log retained for six years, so restored backups never bring back deleted records, while still allowing us to recover past purchase history if an account was closed by mistake."

[RECOMMENDED LAYOUT]:
Side-by-Side Comparison (Left: 99% Nightly Batch | Right: 1% Real-Time Stock Exception)

[SLIDE TITLE]:
Why 99% Daily Batch Reporting is Exactly Right

[SLIDE SUBTITLE]:
Batch vs. Real-Time: What the Business Actually Needs

[SLIDE BODY]:
THE MYTH OF REAL-TIME ANALYTICS:
* Continuous live dashboards across historical sales data create massive server strain and high engineering overhead.
* Executive decisions, marketing reviews, and weekly financial sign-offs do not change minute by minute. Yesterday's numbers, verified and stable, are faster, cheaper, and cleaner.

OUR PRAGMATIC WORKLOAD SPLIT:
1. 99% Daily Batch Reporting (Server 2 at 02:00 AM Nightly):
   * Financial reconciliation, marketing performance, regional analysis, and executive KPIs refresh automatically once every 24 hours at 02:00 AM on Server 2.
   * Completely eliminates daytime reporting load on the live storefront.

2. The 1% Operational Exception (Server 1 Live Stock):
   * Physical warehouse inventory checks remain live on Server 1 during customer checkout so we never oversell items on the shelf.

3. On-Demand Stock Drift Checks:
   * Warehouse supervisors can trigger an on-demand stock check from Server 2 at any time during busy flash sales or payday weekends to catch physical shrinkage without slowing down customer checkouts on the main store.

[SPEAKER NOTES FOR NANA ADJEI]:
"Investors often ask why we are not building streaming, real-time analytics. The truth is that only our physical warehouse stock needs live updates, so customer checkout never sells an item that is out of stock. For everything else (financial totals, marketing metrics, and sales reports), refreshing once a night at 02:00 AM is 100% sufficient. It is rock-solid, affordable, and requires zero complex streaming infrastructure."

[RECOMMENDED LAYOUT]:
Five-Box Grid Layout (Assumptions 1 to 5)

[SLIDE TITLE]:
What We Could Not Verify (Design Assumptions)

[SLIDE SUBTITLE]:
Explicit Assumptions Stated Openly

[SLIDE BODY]:
Because we formulated this strategy with available company records, we explicitly disclose the following design assumptions:

1. REVENUE FORMULA DEFINITIONS:
   * Assumption: We assume Finance reports net paid orders (cleared in bank accounts) and Operations reports gross placed orders.
   * Action: Standardized database formulas will combine both metrics into one verified reconciliation ledger in Quarter 1 and Quarter 2.

2. CURRENT SERVER HARDWARE CAPACITY:
   * Assumption: We assume our single hosting server has standard modern commercial specifications (at least 8 processor cores and 32 gigabytes of RAM).
   * Action: If smaller, activating the pre-approved second server is an emergency priority.

3. PAYMENT GATEWAY SETTLEMENT TIMING:
   * Assumption: We assume third-party card payment processors and mobile money providers settle customer payments on a standard 24-to-48-hour rolling window.
   * Action: Nightly batch reconciliation schedules will align with these settlement windows.

4. NIGERIA PILOT DATA STORAGE:
   * Assumption: We assume Nigeria pilot orders currently write to our central Accra MySQL database (tagged by country code).
   * Action: Under Section 18(2) of Ghana's Act 843, core privacy duties apply wherever data is stored, and commercial expansion will include a simple cross-border transfer agreement under Nigerian privacy rules as advised by legal counsel.

5. CUSTOMER DELETION COMPLAINT AMBIGUITY (REMOVING VS RESTORING):
   * Assumption: We assume the Data Protection Commission complaint regarding a deleted customer's data could represent either an unfulfilled deletion request (requiring complete removal of extra copies) or an erroneous deletion (requiring targeted account restoration).
   * Action: Our architecture satisfies both through pre-load personal data scrubbing and an immutable deletion audit log.

[SPEAKER NOTES FOR NANA ADJEI]:
"A defensible plan states its assumptions openly. We have assumed our server hardware meets standard specifications, that our revenue definitions follow standard e-commerce patterns, that Nigeria pilot data is hosted centrally in Accra, and that our privacy framework must cover both data removal and account restoration. If any of these assumptions change, our architecture adapts without altering our core strategy."
